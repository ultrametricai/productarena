The single flip worth sitting with this week isn't a flip at all — it's three of them in the same arena. Codex overtook GitHub Copilot on 09-02, lost that lead back to Copilot on 09-03, then Copilot reclaimed it again on 09-04 (source: /arena/ai-coding). Layer in that Codex moved +15.7 and Claude Code +14.9 points this week alone, with Copilot adding +9.4 on top (source: /arena/ai-coding), and this reads less like noise and more like three vendors actively re-fighting for the same seat at the same time — a genuinely contested arena, not a settled one.

Behind every arena score sits a founder-process corpus, and it's less finished than the leaderboards suggest: 96 steps in that corpus still need a human, 30 of them judgment or identity work no agent should stand in for, but 66 are just unfilled market gaps where no live product has an honest workaround yet (source: /processes). Concretely, tasks like installing an SDK in a codebase or confirming an upload each show up twice with no covering product, and single-instance gaps like choosing a bank provider or picking a cap table tool sit there too (source: /processes). That's not a knock on any vendor, it's just where the map still has blank space.

If I'm watching one mover into next week, it's Claude Code. A +14.9 point jump to 41.9 is the largest single-arena climb on the sheet, and it lands in the same AI Coding Agents arena that's already seeing Codex and Copilot trade the lead multiple times in a matter of days (source: /arena/ai-coding). A move that size, in a market this actively contested, usually means either a real capability step or a push aggressive enough to reshape who's fighting whom next week.

Our own data has a built-in caveat worth repeating: score history only starts 2026-08-28, so any biggest-mover claim, including the ones above, is being measured against a baseline of a couple weeks, not a season (source: /changelog). On top of that, several of the arenas we'd call close races are close partly because judging is unstable — LLM Evals & Observability, Web Scraping, and Security Scanners each had multiple triple-judged cells come back inconsistent (source: /arena/llm-evals-observability, source: /arena/web-scraping, source: /arena/security-scanners). A 2.5-point gap next to unstable verdicts is not the same claim as a 2.5-point gap next to stable ones.

Scores here only move when evidence and verdicts are re-derived, not on editorial say-so (source: /changelog), which is exactly why a three-way coding-agent scramble and a 66-item gap list are both worth watching, not spinning.
